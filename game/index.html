Who the Warrior Cats game hub is built for

I built the Warrior Cats game hub for anyone who likes the world Erin Hunter made. You don't have to have read every arc, you don't have to be deep in the fandom, and you don't have to play for hours. The point is simple: drop in, click a tile, and have a good ten minutes.

Lifelong Warriors readers

You've followed the series from Into the Wild all the way to whatever arc is current this year. The names, ranks, and clan rules in these games follow the books, so a Bramblepaw you roll here could walk into a ThunderClan meeting without anyone raising an eyebrow.

New fans, first book in

You can still have fun without knowing the full canon. Open Create a Cat, try a clan, see what name rolls out, and decide if the series is your kind of thing. The game will hold your hand through the rank and pattern choices, so you won't feel lost.

Fanfic writers and roleplayers

When you need a side character for chapter four and don't want to spend 40 minutes inventing one, a quick round will hand you a name, a look, and a rank you can paste straight into your notes. I've used it to break a writing block more times than I want to admit.

Artists hunting for a reference

Spin a round, lock in the fur and eye colors that match the cat in your head, and screenshot the card. It's a fast shortcut to a usable visual reference, and the small portrait on the card is just detailed enough to keep you honest on the anatomy later.

Parents and teachers

The games are free, kid-safe, and run in any modern browser. They're a quiet option for a reading corner, a rainy afternoon, or the last ten minutes of class when the lesson is over but the bell hasn't rung yet.

Anyone with five minutes to kill

Sometimes the mood is right for one round and a slightly silly name, like a ginger loner called Pinecone. That's reason enough to click. No achievement to chase, no streak to maintain.
